1. The Problem, Stated Precisely

Ovarian cancer is the deadliest gynecologic malignancy in the United States. The American Cancer
Society projects roughly 21,000 new diagnoses and 12,450 deaths in the U.S. in 2026, against a
lifetime risk of about 1 in 91. The reason it is so lethal is almost entirely a detection-timing problem,
not a treatment problem: five-year survival is roughly 92% when the disease is caught while still
confined to the ovary, but the large majority of cases are still found after the cancer has already
spread, where survival falls to the 30–40% range. This is the single fact that justifies the entire
project — a screening or triage tool only matters if it moves diagnosis earlier in the disease course.
It is important to be precise about why earlier detection hasn't already been solved, because that is
where the real "friction" the competition is asking about lives:

     ●​ Single-analyte CA-125 is not sensitive or specific enough on its own, especially in
          premenopausal women and in non-serous histologies (mucinous, clear-cell, endometrioid),
          and it rises with many benign conditions (endometriosis, fibroids, pregnancy).

     ●​ Large randomized screening trials have already failed to show a mortality benefit from
          annual CA-125 and transvaginal ultrasound in the general population (UKCTOCS and
          PLCO). This is why no medical body currently recommends population-wide ovarian cancer
          screening for average-risk women.

     ●​ The three FDA-cleared multi-marker blood tests that exist today — OVA1 (2009), ROMA
          (2011), and Overa (2016) — were deliberately not cleared as screening tests. They carry an
          FDA-mandated black-box warning restricting their intended use to risk-stratifying a mass a
          physician has already found on imaging, before referral to a gynecologic oncologist.
          Marketing any of them for population screening is off-label.

     ●​ The workflow gap is not "no test exists," it is "no test exists at the point in care where it
          would change a decision that isn't already being made." A test that duplicates what CA-125 +
          ultrasound already does at the adnexal-mass-workup stage adds little. A test that could
          plausibly change practice would need to either (a) beat the existing adnexal-mass triage tests

===== PDF PAGE 02 =====
          head-to-head, or (b) create a new decision point earlier in the pathway — e.g., in symptomatic
          but not-yet-imaged women in primary care — that doesn't exist today.
This reframing matters for how OVAI should be positioned. The prompt asks for "genuine
understanding of the scope of the problem," and the strongest, most defensible answer is: the
bottleneck is not a missing algorithm, it is a missing low-friction entry point into an already-crowded,
well-regulated diagnostic pathway. Sections 7–8 below lay out a concrete plan for that entry point;
Section 4 shows exactly what the current model can and cannot yet claim.

     ●​ Methylation (HRA011451/MethylBERT supplement, blood/cfDNA): cancer vs. healthy —

          methylation carries independent diagnostic information, not that it improves the miRNA
          model.

     ●​ TCGA-OV tumor stage (Stage I/II vs. III/IV, N=299, 22 vs. 277): 78.9% accuracy is below the
          92.6% majority-class baseline (balanced accuracy 53%, AUC 0.49). This is not a
          cancer-detection result and should never be cited as one — it is a separate,
          currently-unsuccessful research question (tumor staging from molecular data) that happens
          to share a data source with the main project.

     ●​ The gated multimodal fusion network (src/models/gated_fusion.py) is an engineering scaffold,
          exercised only against random synthetic tensors in a unit test (scripts/smoke_test.py). It has
          never seen a real patient. It should be described in competition materials as "architecture
          built and unit-tested, awaiting a patient-linked multimodal cohort to train on" — not as a
          working multimodal model.

5. Rigor Already In Place (worth highlighting to

judges)

Not everything here is a gap. Several choices reflect real methodological maturity and are worth
stating explicitly rather than assuming judges will notice them in the code:

     ●​ Canonical evaluators use nested cross-validation (outer 5-fold for the reported metric, inner
          3-fold for hyperparameter selection) with fixed seeds, rather than a single train/test split.

     ●​ A hard rule against fabricating multimodal patient rows across unrelated studies is written
          into the README and enforced by validation checks in the data-loading code.

     ●​ CPTAC proteomics was investigated and explicitly declined as a fusion source because no
          verified patient-level, blood-specimen link to GSE94533 exists — a disciplined "no" that

===== PDF PAGE 08 =====
          many teams under deadline pressure would not have made (see
          reports/cptac_acquisition_audit.md).
     ●​ 58 candidate external GEO cohorts were systematically screened against platform, specimen,
          and phenotype criteria, with the screening logic and rejection reasons saved
          (reports/potential_external_datasets.md, reports/remaining_queue_screen.md).
     ●​ Borderline tumors are kept as their own category rather than being folded into either
          "cancer" or "non-cancer" for convenience — clinically correct, since borderline ovarian
          tumors are a genuinely distinct, non-invasive entity.

6. Downstream Analysis Plan — Phased Roadmap

Phase 0 — Before the submission deadline (days)

     1.​ Pick one canonical headline result and present the full endpoint table from Section 4 next to
          it, not just the best number.

     2.​ Add a calibration plot (predicted probability vs. observed frequency) for the production
          model — currently no calibration check exists anywhere in the repo.

     3.​ Run a decision curve analysis (net benefit vs. threshold probability) for the production model
          against "treat/refer all" and "refer none" baselines. This is the standard way clinicians and
          clinical judges evaluate whether a risk score is actually useful at plausible decision
          thresholds, and it is a much stronger artifact for a healthcare-focused competition than
          ROC-AUC alone.

     4.​ Re-run the frozen 113-patient evaluation with the feature-selection step performed inside
          each outer fold (see OVAI_Errors_Suggestions_and_Notes.md §1) and report that number
          alongside the current one, so the team can honestly say what the signature's performance
          looks like under a stricter protocol.

     ●​ Move the remaining global (pre-split) feature-availability filters inside the training fold in
          the canonical GSE94533 and methylation evaluators.

     ●​ Add an explicit sample/patient-ID overlap audit between GSE94533 and every external cohort
          (GSE106817/113486/113740) — currently assumed, not verified.

     ●​ Formalize the subgroup analysis already started in reports/error_analysis.md (by stage,
          histology, missingness) into a standing report that is regenerated with every model change,

          clear-cell/endometrioid histology below).
     ●​ Build the single canonical run manifest recommended in the team's own

Phase 2 — 3–6 months: closing the external-validation gap

     ●​ Apply for dbGaP phs000897 access now — this is the only currently-identified early-stage
          serum cohort that could serve as genuine independent validation, and controlled-access
          approvals commonly take 4–8+ weeks, so the application timeline should start well before it
          is needed.

     ●​ Source or prospectively collect an HE4-linked cohort. HE4 is a component of both ROMA
          and Overa; without it, OVAI cannot run a head-to-head comparison against the two most
          relevant FDA-cleared competitors, which will be one of the first questions a knowledgeable
          judge or investor asks.

     ●​ Investigate platform harmonization (e.g., ComBat, removeBatchEffect) only once a serum

          applying such corrections without batch labels amounts to fitting noise. If reaching out to
          the GSE94533 authors for original run-batch metadata is possible, that is the most useful
          single request the team could make.

Phase 3 — 6–12 months: real multimodal fusion and longitudinal modeling

     ●​ Only build patient-linked multimodal training rows once a cohort exists where the same
          patient has both serum miRNA and a second modality (methylation, HE4, imaging) drawn at
          the same visit. Until then, keep methylation and TCGA as independent-cohort benchmarks,
          exactly as the project already does.

     ●​ Consider a longitudinal/trend model in the style of the Risk of Ovarian Cancer Algorithm
          (ROCA) used in UKCTOCS — modeling the rate of change of CA-125 (and potentially the
          miRNA signature) over repeated draws in the same patient, rather than a single time point.
          This is a well-precedented idea in this exact disease area and is a natural extension once a
          cohort with repeat draws exists.

Phase 4 — 12+ months: prospective clinical validation

     ●​ Design a prospective single-platform cohort study in the Tier-1 population (women with an
          adnexal mass headed to workup), matching the evidence design that supported
          OVA1/ROMA/Overa, so the resulting data can support a comparable regulatory claim.

7. Regulatory and Reimbursement Pathway (the

"friction" section)

This is where the competition's stated focus — "the friction of getting innovation into the healthcare
system" — is most directly addressed, and it is the section most pitch decks in this space skip
entirely.
Two realistic pathways exist, and they are not mutually exclusive over time:

     1.​ Laboratory-Developed Test (LDT) via a single CLIA-certified reference laboratory. As of the
          FDA's formal rule vacatur on September 19, 2025 (following the March 2025 federal court
          ruling that FDA lacks statutory authority to regulate LDTs as devices), LDTs are regulated
          under CLIA (administered by CMS), not the FDA device framework. This is currently the
          lower-friction, faster path to a real-world clinical offering: OVAI could be validated and
          offered as a send-out LDT from one reference lab without a 510(k)/PMA/De Novo
          submission. The tradeoff is narrower distribution (one lab performs the test) and no FDA
          clearance to point to with payers.

     2.​ FDA in vitro diagnostic clearance (510(k) or De Novo). This is the path OVA1 (2009), ROMA
          (2011), and Overa (2016) all took, and it is the path to broad multi-lab distribution and the
          strongest payer credibility. For an AI/ML-based test specifically, the FDA finalized guidance
          on Predetermined Change Control Plans (PCCPs) in August 2025: a PCCP lets a
          manufacturer pre-specify how the model will be updated after clearance (e.g., periodic
          retraining on new data) so that in-scope updates don't require a brand-new submission each
          time. This directly reduces the "friction" of maintaining an AI-based diagnostic over time
          and should be built into the regulatory strategy from the start rather than retrofitted later.

Reimbursement note: existing payer medical policies for OVA1/ROMA/Overa explicitly restrict
coverage to adnexal-mass workup and explicitly exclude screening asymptomatic women or
monitoring treatment — this is direct, current evidence for why Tier 1 positioning (Section 2) is the
commercially realistic starting claim, and why claiming a screening indication before the evidence
exists would likely be denied coverage even if a lab offered it as an LDT.
Recommended near-term regulatory move: an early, informal engagement with a regulatory
consultant or an FDA pre-submission ("Q-Sub") meeting, even before a full clinical dataset exists, is
standard practice for exactly this reason — it lets the team learn what evidence FDA would want for
a Tier-1 claim before designing the Phase 4 prospective study, rather than after.

8. Workflow and Systems Integration Plan

A model with good statistics that doesn't fit into how a clinic actually works will not reduce
time-to-diagnosis. Concretely:

     ●​ Where it sits: a patient presents with an adnexal mass on imaging (or persistent symptoms
          matching a validated symptom index) → today's order set already includes CA-125 ± HE4 →
          OVAI would run as a reflex or parallel add-on order at that same blood draw, not a new visit
          or a new specimen type.

     ●​ Result delivery: result returned as a structured, discrete value through the existing
          lab-interface pathway (HL7/FHIR order and result messages) so it appears in the EHR the
          same way any other send-out lab result does, rather than requiring a separate portal login for
          the ordering clinician. The standalone Streamlit portal already built is an excellent demo and
          validation tool, but production delivery should target the clinician's existing EHR, not a side
          application.

     ●​ Decision support: rather than a bare "CRITICAL / LOW RISK" label, the result should map
          onto existing SGO/ACOG referral guidance for suspected ovarian cancer, ideally as a CDS
          Hooks-style alert at the point the result lands, so the tool augments a referral decision the
          clinician is already trained to make rather than introducing a new decision framework.

     ●​ Specimen logistics: standard serum tube via the existing phlebotomy workflow, batchable to
          a central or regional reference lab exactly like any other send-out molecular test — no new
          collection device required for the near-term Tier-1 claim.

     ●​ Hardware track (optional, longer horizon): the prompt explicitly allows hardware solutions. A
          point-of-care qRT-PCR cartridge for the 5-miRNA signature is a plausible longer-term
          product but requires materially more capital, its own analytical-validation program, and
          (likely) a full FDA IVD pathway rather than the LDT route. Recommendation: lead the pitch
          with the software/reference-lab track, where the data readiness actually is today, and name
          the point-of-care hardware version as a stated future direction rather than the near-term ask.

found, quantified, and explained its own model's biggest weakness — and has a specific, funded plan
to fix it — reads as more credible than a team presenting only a single high accuracy number, not
less.

Independent QA pass over the OVAI-main codebase, cross-referenced against the team's own

1. Reporting and Framing Findings (new — not in the

internal audit)

1.1 [High] The headline outline number and the canonical benchmark number are
not the same claim

The outline text — "An engineered logistic regression model... achieved a mean out-of-fold ROC-AUC of
0.8997, with 86.43% balanced accuracy, 82.70% sensitivity, and 90.20% specificity for the evaluated Stage I/II
endpoint" — traces to scripts/test_high_capacity_models.py (→ reports/high_capacity_model_results.md),
which evaluates 113 patients: 52 Stage I/II cancers vs. 61 benign/control, with borderline tumors and
Stage III/IV cancers excluded. The project's own canonical evaluator
(scripts/evaluate_gse94533_diagnostic_model.py) reports 0.813 AUC on the full 179-patient cohort. Both
numbers are legitimate, correctly-computed nested-CV estimates — they simply answer different
questions, and the gap between them (0.90 vs. 0.81) is mostly explained by which patients are in the
denominator, not by a better model. Action: always state N and the exact endpoint next to any
percentage in pitch materials; report both numbers side by side (done in the companion
methodology document, Section 4).

1.2 [High] The frozen production signature was likely selected using the same 113
patients it is "validated" on

hsa-miR-296-3p, hsa-miR-6826-5p, hsa-miR-32-3p, hsa-miR-1255a). That exact set closely matches the
miRNAs most frequently selected by the fold-wise L1/elastic-net stability analysis run earlier in the
project on the same 113-patient early-stage-vs-benign/control endpoint
(reports/feature_selection_stability.md, reports/clinical_ablation_results.md). Across the project's own
iterations on this fixed cohort, the reported AUC for this endpoint climbs over successive phases:

Phase 10 (test_high_capacity_models.py), fixed    0.899
signature, no interactions ("frozen_logistic")

Phase 10, fixed signature + 2 interaction terms   0.900
("engineered_logistic", shipped model)

This pattern is consistent with a well-known pitfall in biomarker discovery sometimes called
feature-selection leakage or "double-dipping": if the specific 5 miRNAs used in the final frozen
model were chosen (even indirectly, across several earlier scripts) using label information from this
same 113-patient cohort, then re-running cross-validation on that same cohort with that now-fixed
signature will tend to look better than the signature's true out-of-sample performance, because the
signature was implicitly tuned to this cohort's idiosyncrasies before the "final" evaluation ever ran.
This is distinct from — and additional to — the transductive feature-filtering leakage the team's own
audit already flags.
This is not a claim of misconduct — it is a normal, easy-to-fall-into consequence of running many
exploratory scripts against one small, fixed cohort, and every individual script's internal CV is
implemented correctly. But it means the 0.8997 AUC should not be treated as an unbiased estimate
of how this exact signature will perform on new patients.
Remediation, either of:

     ●​ Re-run feature selection inside every outer fold (as Phases 6–9 already correctly do) using the
          interaction-term feature space, and report that number as canonical instead of the
          fixed-signature version — this gives an honest nested estimate.

===== PDF PAGE 17 =====
     ●​ If the fixed signature is being kept for deployment/interpretability reasons, validate it on a
          cohort that played no role in choosing it. No such cohort currently exists — this is the same
          conclusion as the external-validation gap and reinforces why it is the top scientific priority
          (see methodology document, Section 6).

1.3 [Moderate] The two interaction terms in the production model add ~0.0003
AUC and trade sensitivity for specificity

From the same reports/high_capacity_model_results.md table: the simpler model without interaction
terms ("frozen_logistic": Top-5 signature + Age + CA-125 only) scores ROC-AUC 0.8994, balanced
accuracy 86.6%, sensitivity 84.6%, specificity 88.5%, versus the shipped model with two interaction
terms at ROC-AUC 0.8997, balanced accuracy 86.4%, sensitivity 82.7%, specificity 90.2%. The
interaction terms buy essentially no discrimination and cost ~2 points of sensitivity for ~2 points of
specificity. For a cancer-detection use case, a missed cancer (false negative) is a materially worse
error than an unnecessary referral (false positive), so the simpler, higher-sensitivity model is arguably
the clinically preferable choice, not the one currently in production_artifacts/. Recommend revisiting
which of the two gets deployed, and stating the sensitivity/specificity tradeoff explicitly rather than
only the AUC when justifying the choice.

3. Software Engineering and Reproducibility (new

findings)

3.1 [High] No version control in the delivered project

git log on the delivered codebase returns "not a git repository." There is no commit history, so there
is currently no way to show which exact version of the code produced any given number in a pitch deck,
no diff history, and no way to recover an earlier state if something regresses. Recommend initializing
git immediately, committing the current state, and tagging the exact commit used to generate every
number that goes into competition materials. This is a five-minute fix with outsized credibility value
if a judge or investor asks "can I see how this evolved?"

3.2 [Moderate] No LICENSE file

There is no LICENSE anywhere in the repository. Whether this should be permissive open-source, a
restrictive/proprietary license, or something in between is a business decision for the team (tied to
whether they want to show code publicly on the investor track), but the current state (no license at
all) defaults to "all rights reserved" with no clarity for collaborators or judges who may want to
inspect the code.

3.3 [Moderate] No automated tests beyond one manual smoke script

scripts/smoke_test.py is the only test in the repository, and it only checks that the untrained
multimodal architecture runs and its masking behaves on random tensors — there is no tests/
directory and no pytest usage anywhere. For a project whose core claims rest on "no leakage, no
fabricated multimodal rows," a small suite of automated guardrail tests would materially strengthen

===== PDF PAGE 19 =====
the story: e.g., a test asserting zero patient-ID overlap between GSE94533 and every external cohort,
a test asserting every reported metric's script uses fold-contained preprocessing, a test asserting the
production manifest's feature list matches the model's actual coef_ dimensionality. These are cheap
to write and directly demonstrate the rigor claimed in the reports.

3.4 [Moderate, confirmed and extended] Portability: hardcoded paths

45 of the repository's 100 scripts reference Path.home()/ovarian-ai or similar hardcoded locations rather
than the configs/project.yaml paths block that already exists for exactly this purpose. A judge or
reviewer trying to actually run the delivered zip will hit this on the first legacy script they try.
Recommend routing every script through the existing config paths before the deadline, at minimum
for whichever scripts are named in competition materials as reproducing a specific number.

3.5 [Moderate] Frozen model artifacts have no recorded library versions

production_artifacts/manifest.json records the feature list, transform, and hyperparameters, but not the
scikit-learn / numpy / joblib versions used to fit and serialize frozen_model.joblib. Joblib-pickled
scikit-learn estimators are not guaranteed to load correctly across scikit-learn major-version
boundaries — this is a real, silent-failure risk if the artifact is loaded in a different environment than
it was produced in (e.g., a judge's machine, or a future teammate's laptop). Recommend adding a pip
freeze > requirements-lock.txt alongside every frozen artifact, and recording the exact library versions
inside manifest.json itself.

3.6 [Low] Extensive near-duplicate script variants

There are multiple clean / standalone / final / _by_id variants of the same historical-reproduction and
frozen-signature-transfer experiments (e.g., four separate test_gse8841_signature_on_gse12172*.py
scripts, four reproduce_original_historical/pmml*.py scripts). This is technical debt rather than a
correctness problem, but it makes the delivered zip hard to navigate for anyone outside the team.
Recommend: pick one canonical script per reported result now, move the rest to scripts/legacy/, and
add a short RESULTS_INDEX.md that maps every number appearing in competition materials to
(script, exact command, output file, N, endpoint) — a very fast, very high-value document to write
given how much is already in reports/.

4. Data and Biological Notes

4.1 [Moderate] No HE4 anywhere in current data assets

===== PDF PAGE 20 =====
HE4 is a component of both ROMA and Overa, the two most relevant FDA-cleared comparators. Its
absence means the project currently cannot run a direct, apples-to-apples comparison against either.
This is the single most valuable near-term data-acquisition target — recommend actively sourcing
an HE4-linked serum cohort, or prioritizing HE4 collection in any prospective data-collection effort.

4.2 [Notable, biologically grounded] False negatives cluster in

clear-cell/endometrioid histology, with lower miR-200 family expression

reports/error_analysis.md shows false negatives across every model variant are disproportionately
Endo/Clear Cell histology, and the largest expression contrasts between false negatives and
correctly-classified cancers are consistently in hsa-miR-200c-3p, hsa-miR-200a-3p, and
hsa-miR-29a-3p — all lower in the missed cases. This is not a data artifact; it tracks with published
biology: the miR-200 family is tied to epithelial-mesenchymal-transition signaling that is most active
in high-grade serous carcinoma, and clear-cell/mucinous histologies are already independently
known in the literature to be less well served by CA-125. Recommendation: report performance
stratified by histologic subtype as a standing, first-class metric (not just an appendix analysis), and
name "does this generalize across histologic subtype" as its own explicit open question in
competition materials rather than letting it be smoothed over by the aggregate cancer-vs-non-cancer
number. This is a genuine, well-supported scientific insight worth foregrounding — it will read well
to a judge with clinical background.

4.3 [Low] Stage I cannot currently be evaluated separately from Stage II

The current GSE94533 metadata group Stage I and Stage II together; every attempt to isolate Stage I
alone reports "insufficient class variation." Since Stage I is the highest-value detection target
clinically (the point at which 5-year survival is ~90%+), this is worth flagging as a specific
data-acquisition or metadata-refinement goal rather than leaving it implicit.

4.4 [Low] CA-125 missingness

CA-125 is missing in roughly 60 of 179 patients (~34%); Age is missing in 1 of 179. Imputation is
handled correctly (fold-contained), but given CA-125 is a primary driver of the "combined" model's
lift over miRNA-alone, this missingness rate is worth disclosing prominently rather than only in a
data-quality appendix.

4.5 [Confirmed, restated for emphasis] The multimodal neural network has never

seen real data

src/models/gated_fusion.py is a real, reasonably well-designed architecture (per-modality encoders,
learned gates, explicit missing-modality masking), but it has only ever been run against torch.randn

===== PDF PAGE 21 =====
synthetic tensors in scripts/smoke_test.py. This is legitimate scaffold-testing practice, and the
README is careful to call it a "scaffold" — but any bullet point or slide describing it should say
"engineering scaffold, unit-tested, not yet trained" explicitly, since a reader skimming a feature list
("gated multimodal neural network...") could easily infer real multimodal performance exists.

8. Longer-Horizon Suggestions (not urgent for the

pitch, good research directions)

     ●​ Benchmark against other published circulating-miRNA ovarian cancer panels in the
          literature, not only the project's own historical signature, to contextualize where this
          signature sits relative to the field.

     ●​ Add probability calibration (Platt scaling or isotonic regression) on top of the existing
          logistic pipeline before any clinical framing of the output probability as a literal risk
          percentage.

     ●​ Treat the platform-shift finding (Section 2) as the project's central scientific result, not a side
          note: across three independent external cohorts, ranking ability survives (AUC 0.77–0.83) but
          absolute-scale classification does not — this is a genuinely useful, generalizable finding
          about circulating-miRNA biomarkers across sequencing/array platforms, and is arguably
          more publishable and more interesting to sophisticated investors than the internal accuracy
          number itself.

     ●​ Given how consistently every purely statistical fix attempted so far (rank transforms,
          pairwise ratios, quantile normalization) has failed to rescue external performance, the most
          credible path forward is prospective, harmonized, multi-site data collection rather than
          further post-hoc correction of already-collected, heterogeneous public data. This should be
          the team's central "ask" for both funding and expert clinical collaborators.
